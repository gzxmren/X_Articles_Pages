8 Checks You Should Know Before Copying Polymarket Wallets
1.4万
Did you know? A 100% win rate can be the most dangerous number on Polymarket. Look at this wallet with a 100% win rate and nearly $1M in profit with 400 trades. On paper this looks like an alpha wallet. It is not. Why? This article exposes 8 checks to evaluate a wallet worth copy trading. Let's dive.

Part 1. Eight checks you can do yourself

Two minutes. All on the wallet page. No tools.

1. Age

Where: Top of the page Want: 3 months or more This wallet: 1 day
Under 3 months, you cannot tell skill from a lucky streak. Stop here and open a different wallet.

2. Closed positions, not trades

Where: the Closed Positions tab. Read the number in brackets Want: 50 or more This wallet: 2
Trades and bets are not the same thing. One bet can take many trades to fill. This wallet made 400 trades and only 2 real bets.
A 100% win rate on 2 bets is a coin landing heads twice.

3. Total volume

Where: top of the page Want: $100k or more This wallet: $4.69M. It passes
Volume shows they trade at real size. But read it with check 2. Big volume on 2 bets is one big gamble, not a record.
You can also work out a rough ROI here. Divide PNL by total volume. This wallet: $996k divided by $4.69M is 21%. That looks great, and on 2 bets it still means nothing.
Careful with ROI. This version is profit per dollar traded. It is not profit per dollar they put at risk. Those are different numbers, so never compare an ROI from one place with an ROI from another.

4. The profit graph

Where: the graph. It opens on 1D. Click ALL Want: a steady climb This wallet: one straight jump up in a single day
Look at the shape, not the number. Three shapes:
  • Steady climb. Small steps up over months. This is what you want.
  • One jump. Flat, then a cliff, then flat. That is one lucky bet. It already happened.
  • Climb, then flat. They stopped, or their edge stopped working, or they hold open bets that have not finished. Check 6 tells you which.
A graph that went flat two months ago is not a wallet that is winning now.
Two more things on this graph.
Find the biggest dip. That is their worst run so far. If you copy them, expect to sit through one like it. Size so you can.
Look for flat gaps in the middle. Long quiet stretches mean the record covers less real trading than the age suggests.

5. Categories

Where: the pie chart and the categories panel Want: one or two This wallet: looks like five. It is one bet
Good traders usually do one thing. Someone who trades tennis all year knows tennis. Someone trading tennis, politics, crypto and weather is just busy.
One trap here. In this example all five categories show the same $977,726: Sports, Soccer, Games, UCL, UCL Matchday. Those labels sit inside each other, from wide to narrow. Read the top row only.

6. Active positions

Where: the Active Positions tab Want: open bets that are not deep in the red This wallet: zero open
When you copy someone, you do not get their old profit. You get what they hold right now. A great history plus losing open bets means you take those losses.

7. Trade size and timing

Where: Avg trade at the top, then the Activity tab Want: it should look like a person This wallet: 400 trades in one day
A bot makes many small trades very close together. Even a winning bot is hard to follow. By the time your copy lands, the price moved, and you pay a little every time.
You want a handful to a few dozen trades a day, similar sizes, with real gaps between them.
There is also a floor. A wallet that trades twice a month gives you almost nothing to copy. You will wait weeks to learn if it works for you.
What counts as active depends on the category. A sports wallet should be trading most days. A politics wallet can go quiet for weeks and still be fine, because its markets take months to finish. Just know that you will learn slowly there.

8. Biggest win against total win

Where: the Overview panel. Compare Biggest win to Win Want: biggest win under half This wallet: $683,444 of $977,726. One market is 70% of everything
Take that one market away and almost nothing is left. You cannot copy it, because that market is already finished.
A good wallet's profit is boring and spread out.
Now click Biggest losses on the same panel. This shows you the size of one bad bet, and whether they cut a loser early or held it to zero. You will be copying that habit too.

Two more traps

Both sides of the same market. Open their positions list. If you see YES and NO in the same market, look closer.
If the two prices add up to under $1, that is arbitrage. It is free money for them and worthless to you, because you will not get both halves at those prices. Skip it.
If they add up to over $1, they are protecting a bet they already hold. That is fine to copy. Just copy their exits too, or you keep the half they closed.
Wallets paid to sit still. Polymarket pays daily rewards to accounts that leave orders in the book. Those wallets earn the spread and the reward. Both go to them, not to you. More on this in Part 2.

The two-minute version

Go in order. Stop at the first fail. Most wallets fail in the first thirty seconds.
Anything that passes all eight is worth a real look. That will be a small number of the wallets you open.

Part 2. What no page can show you

Two things decide if a copy trade works. You cannot see either one on any wallet page. Not on Ares, not on Polymarket, not anywhere. Both need the old order book, price by price, going back months.

Does it cross the spread?

Some top wallets never chase a price. They place an order and wait for someone else to come to them.
Your copy buys at market. So you pay the spread they were collecting. Their edge becomes your cost.
On a wallet page this looks perfect. High win rate, smooth profit, small dips. When we run the real test, about 6 in 10 of the wallets that pass everything else fail this one.

What will your price be?

They buy at 50c and sell at 55c. They make 5c.
You buy at 54c and sell at 55c. You make 1c.
Same trade. 80% less profit.
The trap is that this gap is usually zero. We checked 818 real copy fills. Most cost nothing. But 1 in 20 cost 13c a share. Judge the bad fill, not the normal one.

What to expect

We tested 590 wallets over four weeks each. A fully checked wallet is green about 6 weeks in 10.
Four red weeks in ten is normal. Checking carefully moves you from a coin flip to about 6 in 10. That is real. It is not certainty.
For scale, Pew Research looked at 11,989 active Polymarket wallets over six weeks and found 56% lost money.

Three numbers that fool people

  • Win rate. It comes from the price paid, not skill. Only buy 85c favourites and you win 85% of the time and earn nothing.
  • Profit. Sorting by profit finds big accounts and market makers. Often the least copyable wallets on the platform.
  • ROI. Return on what? Return on money used and return on money traded are different numbers. Always ask which.

In short

A profitable wallet is only the starting point.
In two minutes, you can check a wallet’s age, completed trades, volume, overall profit chart, market focus, open positions, and trade timing. Look closely at whether most of its profit comes from a single market.
